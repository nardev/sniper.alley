Martin "Marty" Sugarman was an American photojournalist, sociologist and former professional surfer who left a sun-bleached life on the California coast to document the war in the former Yugoslavia, producing some of the earliest independent photographic records of the siege of Sarajevo and the wider conflict in Bosnia-Herzegovina and Croatia.  

Born in Los Angeles, Sugarman had already lived several lives before he ever picked up a camera on a battlefield — a champion surfer who won the 1965 LA County Surfing Championship, a painter, a radio news director, and co-founder of the cult waterfront-culture magazine H2O. It was not until 1992, after a trip documenting rural poverty in Mexico, that Sugarman — then in his mid-forties — turned his lens toward war. He first traveled to Croatia and Bosnia-Herzegovina that June, and what he saw compelled him to keep returning.  
"I came back here for about three weeks and looked at the film and realized I had to go back," he said of his first trip into the conflict.

"I don't think the American public has a full understanding of the conflict. This is genocide." — Los Angeles Times, "War: Photographer Under Fire in Balkans," Oct. 1, 1992

"The ski jump, everything was there... The Serbians had bombed the soccer fields and the basketball court. They were totally pockmarked with shells, but it was like being in an alpine village. It was beautiful." — describing the 1984 Winter Olympics venues above Sarajevo, now a frontline military position

"God be with you." — the parting words of a Croatian border guard as Sugarman crossed into the war zone; he later used the phrase as the title of his book, God Be With You: War in Croatia and Bosnia-Herzegovina

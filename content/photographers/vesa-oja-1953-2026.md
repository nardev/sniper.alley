Vesa Oja was born in Lahti, Finland, in 1953. He studied photography at the Lahti Institute of Industrial Arts between 1973 and 1976. From 1978, he worked as a staff photographer for Finland's leading national newspaper, Helsingin Sanomat, a position he held until his retirement in 2012.
During his career, Oja became one of Finland's most respected photographers and photojournalists, known for his uncompromising, independent approach to his work. Among his most significant assignments was his coverage of the wars that accompanied the collapse of socialism in Eastern Europe, including the Romanian Revolution of 1989 and, most notably, the Bosnian War, which he documented between 1991 and 2000. His reporting took him into Sarajevo during the city's nearly four-year siege by Bosnian Serb forces — one of the longest sieges of a capital city in modern history — where he photographed civilians enduring daily shelling and sniper fire alongside moments of resilience and ordinary life persisting under extraordinary circumstances. One of his best-known images from this period, taken in December 1994, showed ballerina Marijana Jancec rehearsing at Sarajevo's National Theatre even as the city remained under siege — a photograph that came to be regarded as one of his masterworks and a striking testament to the endurance of cultural life amid war. Helsingin Sanomat later described his work covering the Bosnian conflict as unforgettable. In 1999, Oja published the photo book Toinen Eurooppa – The Other Europe, which addressed the disintegration of socialism in Eastern Europe and the civil wars in the Balkans, drawing heavily on the material he had gathered in Sarajevo and the wider region. In 2013, he completed his long-term project Finglish – Photographs of Finns in North America, which combined photographs and interviews documenting Finnish American and Finnish Canadian communities. Vesa Oja died on 25 February 2026, at the age of 72, following a rapidly progressing and aggressive brain cancer.

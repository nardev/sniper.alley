Gilles Peress was born on 29 December 1946 in France and is considered one of the most influential documentary photographers of his generation and a longtime member of Magnum Photos, which he joined in 1971. Over a career spanning more than five decades, Peress has built a body of work centered on conflict, migration and human rights, documenting events in Iran, Northern Ireland, Lebanon, Rwanda and the former Yugoslavia. Known for his raw, unflinching visual style, he has published influential photobooks including Telex Iran, The Silence: Rwanda, Farewell to Bosnia and The Graves: Srebrenica and Vukovar. Peress's work in Bosnia remains among his most significant. He later returned to the region to document the exhumation of mass graves following the Srebrenica genocide of 1995, working alongside forensic investigators, an experience that resulted in 'The Graves', a harrowing visual record of the massacre's aftermath. During the Siege of Sarajevo, Peress spent extended periods in the besieged city, capturing the daily struggle for survival under relentless sniper fire and shelling. His photographs from this period compiled in 'Farewell to Bosnia' bear witness to the suffering, resilience and devastation endured by Sarajevo's citizens throughout nearly four years of siege, standing as some of the most powerful testimonies of the war.

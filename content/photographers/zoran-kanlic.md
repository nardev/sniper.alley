Zoran Kanlić was a Sarajevo photographer who devoted his life to the camera. Before the war, he worked as a photographer for the Organizing Committee of the 1984 Sarajevo Winter Olympics, shot album covers, collaborated with the German photo agency Bus, served as unit photographer on Ademir Kenović's film Savršeni krug (The Perfect Circle) and was among the first photojournalists for the independent magazines Slobodna Bosna and Dani. As official photographer of Kamerni teatar 55, he documented most of the theatre's wartime productions. When the siege of Sarajevo began in April 1992, Kanlić made a deliberate choice that set his work apart: rather than chase the destruction filling the world's front pages, he photographed the handful of cafés and clubs that stayed open, capturing couples still dancing and friends sharing a cigarette amid the ruins. With no electricity or running water, he developed and printed his negatives by hand in a makeshift darkroom inside his own apartment. The resulting black-and-white photographs, later exhibited as Escape from Reality — Sarajevo 1992–95 and Ratni portreti Sarajeva (War Portraits of Sarajevo), became known for their unflinching honesty about everyday resilience and the loneliness beneath it. Kanlić died in 2020 after a short illness.
